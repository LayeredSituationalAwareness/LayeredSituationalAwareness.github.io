Layered Driver Situational Awareness from Language

Fig. 1: A conceptual illustration of layered Situational Awareness (SA). A driver's situational awareness progresses in stages: from (I) rapid perception of what is where, through (II) comprehension of context and how objects interact with one another, to (III) projection of possible future states.

Abstract

Drivers need to build and maintain sufficient Situational Awareness (SA) of their driving environment in order to drive safely. Therefore, the ability to estimate a driver's SA (or its lack thereof) is critical for effective driving assistance. Traditionally, a driver's SA has been modeled as the ability to recognize objects and spatial layouts. However, SA is layered, and encompasses not only perception and comprehension, but also projection: what might happen next.

To this end, we create a novel dataset with 3,320 annotated layered SA labels from 42 participants observing a diverse set of driving videos in a supervisory control context. We extract layered SA labels directly from participants' natural language responses to SAGAT-style probes. Our analysis of how awareness varies due to high-level features about the gaze scanpaths, object properties, and distractions, uncovers systematic patterns in the depth and dynamics of SA.

We also propose a simple and intuitive baseline to predict an observer's SA from gaze scanpaths, providing a first step towards computational modeling of layered SA. We will release the code and dataset publicly.
